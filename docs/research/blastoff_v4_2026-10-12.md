# Blast Off v4: research and build plan (2026-10-12)

This is a plan only. Zeke (10-12): *"let's still not make anything just yet, let's keep planning."*
No scene code was changed and nothing heavy was rendered. The small headless tests named below live in
`scratch/scenes/blastoff4_research/`. Every one was launched through `scripts/server/render.sh`, and each
ran for under 2 s.

The base is v3, `scripts/lyric_scenes/real/rocket3/build_scene.py` (accepted), with args from
`scratch/scenes/blastoff3_env.sh`. Every music reaction is baked as keyframes from
`state/tzeke_songs/blast_off/timeline_30fps.npz` (175.8 bpm, 30 fps, 5976 frames, 1 bar = 1.365 s).
There are no handlers and no Python drivers. **v4 keeps that rule.** Each technique below is either
static geometry plus keyed scalars, or numpy-precomputed motion baked to keys. Renders are chunked and
resume mid-timeline (`render_frames.py`), so **nothing may depend on simulation history** that
isn't baked first.

## 2. Item 1: LED strips/rings strobing rainbow + a spectrum panel on the rocket

### What v3 does
`LED_OBS` (line ~1257) has 6 core rings, 4 core strips (one object), 3 rings per SRB, 2 on S2, and the nose. Section 10b
(line ~3431) keys **per-object `obj.color`**, which the shader reads through Object Info. One flat colour per ring is
taken from a 5-colour palette that changes every 2 bars. So a ring is one colour at a time, with no motion along it.

### v4 technique: one LED-pixel shader, global keyed scalars
Replace `M_LED`/`M_LEDHOT` for the rocket (and later the tower and speaker) with **`LEDPixel`**:

- **Coordinates without UVs:** `ShaderNodeTexCoord.Object`. A ring's position is `u = atan2(y, x)/2π` (`ShaderNodeMath`
  `ARCTAN2`) and a strip's is `u = z / length`. `obj.color` keeps its per-object role, now holding a phase offset and a
  gain (R = phase, G = gain, B = "kind").
- **Discrete diodes:** `d = abs(fract(u·N_LED) − 0.5)` → `smoothstep(0.32, 0.18, d)` gives dots, with dark "PCB" between them.
  N_LED is about 4 per metre (giant LEDs on a 100 m rocket), so the dots read in close chase shots.
  **Pitfall (moiré):** at range the dots alias. Fade the dot mask to its mean (≈0.45) using `ShaderNodeCameraData` →
  View Distance, mapped 150 m → 600 m.
- **Rainbow chase:** hue = `fract(u·k + BEATS·speed + phase)` → `ShaderNodeCombineColor` (HSV mode) → emission.
  **`BEATS` must be keyed MONOTONIC** (beat index + `T_ph`) and wrapped with `fract` *in the shader*. A keyed 0→1 sawtooth
  with LINEAR keys interpolates through the wrap on motion-blur sub-frames, which causes a one-frame colour smear on every beat.
- **Strobe:** white flash = `STROBE·HOT`, plus a kick flash `KICK` (both already in section 1), mixed toward white.
- **Alarm:** `ALARM` 0→1 (keyed, eased over half a bar at 43.77 s) mixes rainbow → red. Level = `0.25 + 1.4·KICK`, plus a
  half-bar alternate-halves blink (`fract(BEATS/2) < 0.5` vs `u < 0.5`) so it reads as a *warning pattern*, not just red.
  **AgX pitfall:** under "AgX - Medium High Contrast", a bright saturated red rolls to orange/yellow-white. Keep the alarm
  emission moderate (Strength 3–6, colour (1, 0, 0.04)) and check with stills.
- **Keys:** about 6 global scalars as `ShaderNodeValue` in the shared material, baked with the existing
  `bake(M.node_tree, value_path(...))`. That's 6 F-curves instead of 3 per LED object. Per-object `obj.color` only
  changes at staging events.

### Spectrum panel ("audio-visualizer panel on the rocket")
**The timeline's `bars` array is not usable as-is**, which I measured. Columns 0, 2, 3, 5 and 8 are always 0, because
`lyric_viz.py` uses 40 log bands from 40 Hz on a short FFT, so the low bands get no bins. The low columns also saturate
(27 % of frames at ≥0.99 in column 1).
A 32-band mel spectrum works cleanly on this song: **0 empty bands, about 2 % saturation per band**
(`librosa.feature.melspectrogram(n_fft=4096, hop=1600 @ 48 kHz, n_mels=32, fmin=30, fmax=16000, power=1)` → dB →
per-band p5/p98 normalisation). Add the fast-attack/slow-release envelope from the `blender-audio-reactive` skill §2, a peak-hold
cap (hold 6 frames, fall 1.5 rows/frame), and a gamma (~1.6) so the bars don't sit high (the means came out 0.5–0.78).
Write it to `state/tzeke_songs/blast_off/spectrum32_30fps.npz` (a new file).

Recommended rendering, the **"audio texture"**:
- `bpy.data.images.new("Spectrum", 32, N, float_buffer=True)` with R = level and G = peak cap, then `pixels.foreach_set`
  and **`img.pack()`** so render workers have it. Colourspace `Non-Color`, Image Texture `interpolation='Closest'`.
  5976 rows is under the 16384 texture limit, and the image is about 3 MB.
- The panel shader samples `(col+0.5)/32, (floor(FRAME)+0.5)/N`. `FRAME` is a keyed Value = IDX. `floor` keeps
  motion-blur sub-frames on one row. A cell is lit if `row/16 < level`. Colour runs green → yellow → red by row
  (all red while `ALARM`), with the LED-dot mask on the cells.
- The panel is one flat object on the core stage between two strips, facing −Y (the camera side), about 6 × 10 m with a
  black glossy bezel. Parent it to `S1`.
- **The same image drives** the speaker's side matrices (item 4, replacing v3's 24 VU box objects), an "EQ mode" for
  the core strips (strip k fills to band k), and the HUD's mini spectrum. It's one source of truth.

Alternative considered: GN `GeometryNodeSampleSoundFrequencies` (exists in 5.2.1, verified). It's live, with no bake.
It was rejected because it has no envelope or peak hold, ties the render to the audio file, and breaks the
"everything baked" rule.

Hooks: lines ~1258–1290 (materials + `led_ring`); colour bake loop line ~3438. Cost: emissive only, ~0 %.

## 3. Item 2: LED lights on the launch tower and pad

The NASA ML is `lib_obj("ML")` (line ~1929). Its library bounds are x −15..33, y −20..20, z −7..110 m, with 19 k polys,
rotated 90° in scene. There are sodium work lamps every 8.5 m (`WORKL`, line ~2003), beacons and searchlights (line ~2024),
and three 180 m lightning towers (`LT_POS`).

Plan:
- **Find the tower footprint from the mesh**, not by hand: take ML vertices with local z > 15 m, cluster their x/y
  extremes, and get 4 corner columns. Add vertical LED strips (box 0.25 × 0.25 m) on the 4 corners, plus a
  horizontal band at every work-lamp level (`zz`), plus the deck perimeter and the trench lips. Add LED rings every 20 m
  on the lightning towers, reusing their `beam()` geometry positions. All use `LEDPixel`, with `kind` = tower.
- **Behaviour:** before liftoff, a slow breathing cyan/violet with hi-hat strobes (the beacons already strobe on `HAT`,
  line ~3342), and a chase that quickens with `PRE`. Blackout on `T_black` (existing `pad_black`). On liftoff, 3 frames of
  white strobe, then **level k lights when `ALT` passes its height**, so the tower lights follow the rocket up.
- **Spill:** 4 coloured area lights (one per tower face, 20 × 100 m, `use_shadow=False`, `volume_factor` ~0.5),
  keyed to the LED colour. This tints the launch steam VDB. **Cost:** pad shots are already the most expensive part
  (19–39 s/frame, measured), and each light is evaluated in the froxel volume. Budget +5–15 % there (estimate). Start
  with `volume_factor` low.
- Optional, cheap: retint the 3 sky searchlights per bar and swing them on bars. They're already volumetric spots
  (line ~2026).

Hooks: geometry right after the ML block (line ~2030); keys next to the pad-lights section (line ~3323).

## 4. Item 3: THREE side boosters, B3 fails, explosion close by, wobble and recovery

### 4a. The third booster
- **Layout (Q2):** recommend **120° slots**. Keep `BoosterL` at 180°, so the onboard `B4_tail` and `D2_under` shots
  stay valid. Move `BoosterR` to +60°. **B3 goes at −60° (x+, y−)**, facing the south-side cameras, so the failure plays
  toward the lens. Implement with slot empties: `RIG → Slot{k} (rot Z) → Booster{k}`, and rotate the existing camera
  offsets that reference ±`SRB_X` (`B4_tail`, `D2_under`, `F1_sep`, `booster_pos`) through the slot matrix.
  The cheaper alternative is a T-layout (B3 at −Y, 90° spacing). It's asymmetric and less believable.
- Mesh: `lib_obj("SRB_Body", B3, copy=True)` plus a copy of `SLS_SRBatt_R` (130 k polys, sharing mesh data) rotated into
  the slot. Add `plume_pair("b3", B3, "boost3", …)` (line ~1330), 3 more `LEDSrb` rings, and give the plume loop
  (line ~3187) a per-booster `on_b3` mask, so B3 can sputter and die independently of `on_boost`.

### 4b. The failure motion (numpy, baked)
- 38.33 s: `on_b3` gets snare-locked dips (`1 − 0.35·SNARE`) and the B3 plume length jitters. A small leak:
  reuse the v3 `spark_tree()` GN at B3's aft attach.
- 49.23 s, **early sep**, how a real strut failure would go: the aft strut lets go first, so B3 **pivots about its forward
  attach** (tail swings out 10–25° in ~0.6 s). Then the forward attach rips, so it translates out at 8–15 m/s with tumble
  ω ≈ 60–120°/s. **It keeps thrusting**, which drives the cartwheel and the corkscrew smoke path. Add separation-motor puffs
  (the v2/v3 BSEP puff material) at the nose and aft.
- Its path is a closed-form/numpy integration (thrust along its own axis + tumble), so it's deterministic.
- **The corkscrew smoke trail** uses the same "static geometry along a precomputed path" trick as the asteroid trail
  (§6): ~40 instanced puffs (the existing `volume_material(…, shape="SPHERE")`) placed along B3's path, with radius and
  opacity growing with age. Age comes from where the booster *was* when it passed each puff, computed in numpy and
  keyed per puff. That's cheaper than another VDB.

### 4c. The explosion (57.6 s)
- **Asset:** the JangaFX **"Mid-Air Explosion"** VDB (CC0, 1.14 GB, 126 frames):
  `https://www.mediafire.com/file/uwmxisgj9xagrdh/Midair_Explosion_01.rar/file` (link scraped from the JangaFX page).
  ⚠ It's a **.rar**, and the server has **no 7z/unrar/bsdtar** (checked), so it needs an extractor (Q3). The no-install
  alternative is **"Aerial Explosion"** (.zip, 6.6 GB, 120 frames). The fallback is the Ground Explosion we already have,
  but it's already used 3× in v3 (launch steam, early steam, asteroid fire, line ~2246/2252/3674), so a fourth use will read
  as a repeat. If it's the fallback, flip it, rotate it, time-stretch it, and don't let it share a shot with the steam.
- **Shading:** solid-propellant (aluminised APCP) burns **white-yellow with WHITE Al₂O₃ smoke**
  (`blender-fire-smoke-realism` §8). Use `smoke_material(color≈0.85, glow_grid="flames", glow_col=blackbody(3000))`.
  Don't copy v3's dark asteroid smoke (0.3), which is correct for a rock and wrong for an SRB. Import with
  `vdb_seq()` + `vdb_play()` (existing helpers). Print `vol.grids` and check the bounds before placing it (domain-corner
  gotcha, skill §3). Set `fo.data.render.precision="HALF"`.
- **Read:** a 2–4 frame overexposed flash (point light like `BoomFlash`, about 2e9 W, 3000 K, τ ≈ 0.2 s, on B3's side of the
  stack), exposure pop (+1.0 on `expo`, line ~3413), and a **condensation shock shell**: a transparent white sphere with
  fresnel emission/alpha expanding at ~300 m/s for 4–8 frames, then gone. That shell is what makes an explosion read as
  *close*. Add the existing blast-ring trick, plus 5–8 **casing segments**: bisect the SRB mesh into ring bands with
  bmesh (`bisect_plane`), tumbling outward. Burning propellant chunks get short emissive streaks (§6 trail shader). Use
  scorch: key the existing core `Soot` value up on B3's side.
- **Cost:** the climb set already runs about 5 s/frame (measured). A hero VDB near the lens in EEVEE wants `vol_tile` 2
  (add the new shot names to `FINE_TILE`, line ~3935) and a tight `vol_start/vol_end` slab. Budget **2–3× for ~150 frames**
  (+25–40 min of worker time, estimate). VRAM is fine on the V100 at HALF precision.

### 4d. Wobble and recovery
- **Structure:** insert **`WOB` empty: `RIG → WOB → S1, S2, Slot*`**, pivoting at the vehicle centre of mass (rocket-local
  z ≈ 40 m). Shot functions call `wl()`/`wl_lag()` = `rig_matrix` **without** WOB, so tracking and chase cameras do not
  follow the wobble, and it reads on screen. Add `wl_v()` = rig × WOB for **onboard** cameras (they're bolted on, so they must
  wobble) and for HUD reticle targets.
- **Motion** (t from the explosion):
  `pitch(t) = A·e^(−ζωt)·sin(ωt)`, `yaw(t) = 0.7A·e^(−ζωt)·sin(1.3ωt + 1.1)`, roll kick `R·(1−e^(−t/0.3))·e^(−t/3)`.
  Use A = 12–16°, **ω = 2π / 1 bar (1.365 s)** so the swings land on downbeats, and **ζ ≈ 0.07** so the amplitude falls to
  ~10 % by the 65.1 s calm. Add a lateral shove away from the blast, `Δ = 15 m·(1 − e^(−t/0.4))`, that guidance reels back
  over ~6 s. From the 49.23 s sep onward, add a small permanent 2° trim (thrust imbalance) that the wobble rides on.
  Optional realism: counter-gimbal the core plume objects by −0.3× the wobble.
- **Camera shake:** add `EXPL = 2.5·e^(−t/0.35)` (impulse) + `0.6·e^(−t/3)·rumble` into `RAVE_SHAKE` (line ~2632).
  The existing camera loop already scales jolts down for long lenses (line ~3046).
- **"Barely made it":** PITCH (line ~2556) gets a dip during the wobble (the nose falls 6–8° below the nominal programme),
  then recovers by 66.97 s.

## 6. Item 6: debris must bounce off or crumble on the speaker (never pass through)

### Rigid-body test (`t_rigidbody.py`, Blender 5.2.1, headless)
Setup: a kinematic (animated) passive box the size of the v3 speaker (24 × 17 × 38 m), 4 active convex-hull chunks
(4–12 m) fired at it at 120–400 m/s, zero gravity. The bake **works headless** with
`with bpy.context.temp_override(scene=sc, point_cache=rbw.point_cache): bpy.ops.ptcache.bake(bake=True)`
(`{'FINISHED'}`, 0.02–0.04 s). But:
- **Blender rigid bodies have no initial-velocity property.** Injecting speed by animating the chunk as kinematic for
  2 frames and then switching it off gave **inconsistent, substep-dependent velocities**. Some chunks drifted *away* from
  the speaker, and a 230 m/s chunk ended up moving about 0.2 m/frame.
- **Tunnelling:** the 400 m/s chunk **passed straight through** the 17 m-thick box at 10 and 30 substeps/frame
  (Bullet is discrete, and Blender exposes no CCD). That is exactly the failure Zeke saw.
- Results change with `substeps_per_frame`. A cache is also one more thing chunked renders must have baked first.

### Recommendation: analytic collision in numpy, baked to keys
- For each chunk: position, velocity, angular velocity, bounding radius r. Integrate in numpy with **8 substeps per
  frame** over the frames after `BOOM`.
- **Collider = the speaker's oriented box at that frame**, from the already-computed `spk_loc/spk_rot/spk_scl`
  (section "RAVE world plan", line ~2640), including its keyed recoil velocity. Test sphere vs OBB (closest point on the box):
  if the distance is less than r, push the chunk out along the normal n and reflect the *relative* velocity:
  `v' = v − (1+e)(v_rel·n)n`, with e ≈ 0.35. Damp the tangential part by friction 0.3 and add spin from the tangential slip.
- **Crumble:** if the impact speed is over ~60 m/s and r > 6 m, retire the chunk at the contact point and spawn 3–5 fragments from
  a **pre-allocated hidden pool** (the `show()` mask handles visibility). Fragment radius = r × U(0.3, 0.5), velocity =
  reflected v × U(0.4, 0.8) + a spread cone. Add a dust puff (GN points with expanding radius) and sparks, and give the
  speaker a hit reaction: an LED flash near the impact point (keyed `HitAmt` + `HitPos` into the speaker LED shader) and
  an impulse added to `spk_loc`, so it visibly flinches but holds.
- **Collide with the rocket stack too, for free:** use a capsule collider for the core during the B3 explosion debris (§4c).
- **Verification built in:** after the solve, assert that no chunk centre is inside OBB + r on any substep. Print the
  minimum clearance per chunk and fail the build on any violation (this implements CLAUDE.md rule 4).
- Seeded rng, so the result is deterministic. Computing 60 bodies × ~1000 frames × 8 substeps takes well under a second, and the render cost is 0.
- **Aim some debris AT the speaker** so the bounce/crumble is actually seen. A geometric check of v3's seed
  (`v3_chunk_check.py`, rng 1964) says **none of the 5 "return" chunks crosses the cabinet box**. With the chunk radius
  included, they clear it by about 16–80 m. The check approximates the cabinet axes as SIDE/UPV and ignores the kick recoil. So what Zeke saw may be a near-miss read through the 20 mm `M5_hero` lens, or something I didn't model.
  I couldn't confirm it on a contact sheet of frames 4930–5162 (Q5).

Hook: replace the chunk loop at line ~3647 (`for k in range(30)`).

## 7. Item 5: asteroid trail that reads as FAST

Facts: v3 flies the rock on a **straight line** `A_START → A_HIT` (11.6 km in 15.5 s ≈ **750 m/s**), then a slow creep along the
same `V_IN` (line ~2622). A straight path means a static object along it is exact.

What real fast meteors look like (American Meteor Society FAQ + observer reports): a bright head, then a **luminous
"train"** (energised air) that usually lasts seconds, rarely minutes, and is associated with fast meteors. Colours run
from red to blue, and **green/white** is very commonly reported, with heads flashing green→white/blue. A separate
non-luminous smoke trail sits lower and lasts longer. In vacuum there would be no train, so this is artistic licence (Q6).

Plan (all static geometry + one keyed scalar `S_HEAD` = the head's distance along the path):
1. **Trail tube:** one long cylinder mesh along the path line (start → stop, ~12 km), with s (metres along the path) stored as an
   attribute or `Object` z. The shader uses `d = S_HEAD − s`: core = `exp(−d/600 m)·(d>0)`, halo = `exp(−d/2500 m)`, and
   nothing ahead of the head. A cross-section soft edge comes from Layer Weight Facing. Colour: white-hot core → green/cyan
   halo, or tie the halo to the bar's rave `HUE`. Use a second, 3× wider tube for the faint persistent train.
2. **Ablation head:** in the rock material, forward-hemisphere emission = `max(0, dot(N_world, −V_IN))^3 × speed`.
   `V_IN` is a constant vector, and speed is a keyed Value that falls to ~0 when it's pinned at `FIRE`. Add a bow-shock
   shell: a slightly larger, stretched sphere, fresnel emission, open at the back.
3. **Puffs/sparks:** a GN tree: `Mesh Line` along the path → `Mesh to Points` with attribute `s_i`.
   Delete where `s_i > S_HEAD`; scale = `r0 + k·(S_HEAD − s_i)`; `Instance on Points` low-poly icospheres. Sparks are
   jittered points with a small lateral drift ∝ age (`GeometryNodeInstanceOnPoints`, `GeometryNodeStoreNamedAttribute`,
   all verified present in 5.2.1).
4. **The speed cue that matters most: near-field dust motes.** Scatter ~3000 static points within ±300 m of the path.
   When the camera tracks the rock, they streak with the existing motion blur (EEVEE blurs camera motion). Static stars
   don't help, because at infinity they have no parallax.
5. After `FIRE`, key a global `TrailFade` to 0 over ~2 s.

Why not a Simulation Zone trail: it needs history, so a baked cache in front of chunked renders, and the path is already
known in closed form. Cost: emissive surfaces + ~a few hundred instances, estimate +2–5 % in the L-shots.
Hook: after the asteroid block (line ~3630).

## 8. Item 4: a more realistic, more LED, more reactive speaker + strobe lasers

### Cabinet
Recommend a **hybrid**. Take the CC-BY **"Stage Speaker - black" by Sousinho** (downloaded; 6,940 tris, 4K PBR in the
glTF zip, two woofers + horn + grille, front **+Y**). Normalise it to v3's 16 m-tall cabinet at scale 1:
factor **16/259 ≈ 0.0618**, which keeps v3's 0.3→2.4 scale keys and puts it at 38 m. Rotate 180° about Z so the front is −Y as v3 expects.
Then:
- Keep v3's animated **lathe cones** (they move for excursion) at the asset's measured woofer centres (woofer islands at
  y≈46–55, z≈−14…22 and 74…110 in raw units). Hide the asset's static cone islands; it has 152 loose islands, so "separate
  by loose parts" works.
- Use the asset's 4K base colour / metal-roughness / normal, with albedo checked against the PBR rules (`blender-photoreal-lookdev` §2).
  For a grille close-up, ambientCG **SheetMetal001–004** (CC0, perforated, with opacity) can replace the grille.
- ⚠ 6,940 tris is low for a 38 m hero seen from 60–115 m. Check rim faceting on a still. Add a subdivision
  modifier on the cabinet only if it shows (Q4).

### More LEDs, more reactive
- Discrete-LED rings around each woofer and the horn, and an LED pixel frame on the edges (the `LEDPixel` shader, §2).
- **Side LED matrices** (16 × 24 cells each), sampling the spectrum image, replacing the 24 VU box objects (line ~3486).
- A **strobe blinder** bank on top: 2 × 4 white emissive cells flashing on `STROBE·HOT` and kicks during `FIREWIN`.
- **Moving heads:** 4× the CC-BY "Moving Head Beam (high Poly)" by Lord Henry (downloaded; 20,481 tris, real-world
  0.38 × 0.31 × 0.48 m, base/head/support separate), scaled ×8 (~4 m) along the top, with pan/tilt keyed on bars and
  their beams = the laser tree with a wider radius.
- Reactions on top of v3: a cabinet "breath" (scale 1 + 0.01·KICK), a grille shimmer (normal strength ∝ KICK), and debris hit
  flashes (§6).
- Optional silhouette (Q4): stack two CC-BY **"Bass Bin 1 - Subwoofer" (darksoundlab, 7c0d85ad…, 9.5 k faces)**
  underneath (not downloaded).

### Lasers (tested: `t_laser.py`, `t_laser_e.py`, 960×540, 32 spp)
| Variant | Render | Look |
|---|---|---|
| speaker only | 0.24–0.28 s | — |
| 4 GN fans × 16 beams, **additive blended** (Emission + Transparent, `BLENDED`) | 0.67–0.69 s (**≈ +170 %**) | good |
| same beams, **opaque emission** (default `DITHERED`, no transparency) | **0.27 s (≈ +12 %)** | **practically identical** after Fog Glow |
| 4 **volumetric spot lights** (0.6° cone) in world haze (tile 2, density 0.002, range 0.5–6000 m) | 0.32 s | **beams don't resolve**: faint broken specks |

So: **GN-instanced opaque emissive cylinders**, with the halo coming from the compositor glare that's already there
(`Fog Glow`, threshold 1.4, line ~3812). The `LaserFan` GN tree, verified, works like this:
`GeometryNodePoints(Count)` → `GeometryNodeInstanceOnPoints` (a unit `GeometryNodeMeshCylinder`, 6–8 verts, base at origin
along +Z). Rotation = `(index/(N−1) − 0.5)·Spread + Sweep`. `Store Named Attribute` (`FLOAT_COLOR`, domain `INSTANCE`,
"beam_col") is read in the shader with `ShaderNodeAttribute` type `INSTANCER`. Keyed modifier inputs: Count, Spread, Sweep,
Length, Radius, Color, Intensity, and a chase mask `step(fract(BEATS·rate − idx/N))`. On 5.2 the path is
`modifiers[...].properties.inputs.Socket_N.value`, and the build already has `gn_input_path()`.
- **Pitfall: sub-pixel beams shimmer and vanish under TAA.** At 1000 m with a 24 mm lens, one pixel ≈ 0.78 m. Key the
  radius per frame as `max(0.3 m, 1.2 px × camera distance)` from the shot's camera position.
- How real rave lasers look (Pangolin, ILDA, LaserFX): beam effects only show where haze scatters them. The shapes are **fans**
  (beams from one aperture), **sweeps**, **cones/tunnels** (circle scans), **sheets** (fast scans that read as a
  plane), and chases. Colours are pure laser primaries, with green brightest; mixes give yellow, cyan and white. Beams **blank** on
  the beat. Modes to build: fan (default), sweep on bars, tunnel aimed at the asteroid on `FIRE`, sheet on the big-drop
  downbeats (Count 48, low intensity), and hi-hat blanking strobes.
- Under AgX a bright saturated beam core goes white with colour in the glow. That's how photographed lasers look, so it's fine.
- **Vacuum: real lasers are invisible in space with no haze** (Q6). Optional: `GeometryNodeRaycast` against the asteroid
  for exact hit spots + spark bursts + a small point light at each hit.
- Photosensitivity: lasers + strobes + red alarm pulses mean the publish should carry a flashing-lights warning (skill note).

Hooks: the speaker block (line ~3450–3527) and a new laser block after it. Cost ≈ +10 % in speaker shots (scaled from the test).
